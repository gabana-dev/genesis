# The name

**The product is Isobath. The engine is Genesis.**

Decided 2026-08-21. `isobath.io` registered against `whois` returning *Domain not found*, and a
company search returning nothing at all — no business anywhere trades under the name.

---

## What an isobath is

A contour line on a nautical chart joining points of **equal depth**. It is what a navigator
reads to know where the seabed comes up to meet them — not where they are, but how much water
they have left.

That is the product. A leveraged position's liquidation price is a depth. The distance to it is
how much water is under the keel. And the thing we measure that nobody else does — free
collateral — is whether there is any way to gain depth once the bottom starts rising.

**Genesis is the laboratory. Isobath is the instrument it built.** Genesis stays internal: the
repo, the engine, the research program that produced the findings. The public product is Isobath.

## Why this name and not a clearer one

Three filters had to pass at once, and almost nothing survives all three.

**1. The good domain must be genuinely free.** Not "some leftover TLD". Every meaningful English
word is gone on both `.com` and `.io` — verified, not assumed: `buttress`, `keelson`, `plumbline`,
`leeway`, `stanchion`, `shoring`, `untenable`, `waterline`, `ballast`, `assay`, `loadline`,
`holdfast`, `deadweight`. All taken. Squatters hold every pronounceable `.com`.

**2. No existing company.** This eliminated the two best-sounding candidates. *Freeboard* and
*Plimsoll* both had their real domains held. *Loadmark* — a near-perfect fit, the mark showing
safe loading — has **five** companies including an active logistics firm on `loadmark.com`.
*Scantling*, the best semantic fit of all, is buried by an NFL receiver named Valdes-Scantling.

**3. It must not be buried.** This is the filter that rules out the obvious names. A dictionary
word ranks behind Cambridge, Merriam-Webster and Wikipedia forever. A distinctive token ranks
first for itself within weeks — and for AEO it matters more, because a model citing us needs a
name that resolves to exactly one thing.

Isobath passes all three. Searching it returns the cartographic definition and nothing else.

## The cost, stated

**It does not explain itself.** Nobody arrives knowing what an isobath is, and the name says
*depth*, not *defence*. That burden falls entirely on the tagline, which therefore has to work
harder than the name does:

> **Isobath — how much water you have left.**

The alternative was `freecollateral.com`, which explains itself completely and was available. It
was rejected because a category term is not a brand: it cannot be owned, cannot be defended, and
reads like a glossary entry. A name that needs one line of explanation is a better asset than a
name that needs none and belongs to everyone.

## What it lets us grow into

Deliberately not tied to liquidation, Hyperliquid, or crypto. Depth-to-the-bottom generalises to
positioning, crowding, funding and liquidity risk, and to other venues, without a rebrand. The
engine already extends that way; the name had to as well.
