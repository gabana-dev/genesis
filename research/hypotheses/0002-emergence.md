## Current Observations

*Seed note, captured 2026-08-06 — not yet a formed hypothesis. Everything else in this
file remains unwritten; this is a candidate concept held for later investigation, not a

A conversation about heterarchy, hierarchy, and holarchy surfaced a candidate framing for
how emergence might work in Genesis.

Heterarchy: many nodes, no fixed ranking, dominance shifts with context — no single
controller.

Hierarchy: fixed nested control, higher levels govern lower ones.

Holarchy (Koestler; later Wilber): nested wholes that are also parts ("holons"). A cell is
a whole made of organelles, and also a part of a tissue. Interactions within a given scale
tend to be heterarchical — self-organizing, no commander — while the coherent wholes those
interactions produce become, in turn, parts of a still-larger self-organizing system. This
is offered as one candidate resolution of "nature prefers heterarchy" versus "we live in
hierarchy" — not necessarily the only one.

Two places in the existing Canon already reject pure command hierarchy without naming
what replaces it:

`vision.md`, "The Role of Multiple Cognitive Processes": *"Genesis is unlikely to emerge
from a single monolithic model... The project does not assume that intelligence resides
inside any individual component. It may emerge from the quality of interaction between
them."*

`research/conceptual-landscape.md`, Layer 7 — Emergence: *"Genesis does not assume that
intelligence resides within a single module, model, or algorithm."*

Neither document says what kind of structure replaces hierarchy. Holarchy is a candidate
answer, not yet tested against alternatives.

The open fork this raises, not yet resolved: do Genesis's cognitive processes have real
autonomy — each one a whole in its own right, as a holon would require — or is "process"
doing lighter work than that, closer to a heterarchical network with no meaningful
internal wholeness at each node? These would be different architectures.
