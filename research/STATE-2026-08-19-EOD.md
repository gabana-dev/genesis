## 1. Processes: what to restart after the reboot

**Both recorders were stopped cleanly with SIGTERM before the reboot.** Verified: final
checkpoints written, last line of each log parses as JSON, no truncation.

**`--seconds` is the run DURATION and defaults to 1800 — thirty minutes.** Omitting it does not
mean "run indefinitely", it means "stop in half an hour". This doc's first version omitted both
the symbol and the duration and so misled the restart **twice**: q5 died instantly on a usage
error, and hl1 ran for exactly 30 minutes and exited with `"stop_after reached"` before anyone
noticed. Each recorder's declared duration is recoverable from its own first RECORDER event
(`duration_seconds`), which is how both were found.

| | declared duration | declared close | restart with |
|---|---|---|---|
| q5 | 604800 s (7 d) from 2026-08-18T15:44Z | **2026-08-25T15:44Z** | `run.py spot-perp <log> BTCUSDT --seconds <remaining>` |
| hl1 | 1814400 s (21 d) from 2026-08-19T08:31Z | **2026-09-09T08:31Z** | `run.py hyperliquid <log> BTC --seconds <remaining>` |

Compute `<remaining>` to the declared close, never a fresh full duration — restarting with the
original value would run past the boundary the contract declared:

```
.venv/bin/python -c "from datetime import datetime,timezone; \
print(int((datetime(2026,8,25,15,44,tzinfo=timezone.utc)-datetime.now(timezone.utc)).total_seconds()))"
```

Both **append** to their existing files. Two costs, both measured on 2026-08-19: the gap itself, and a **replay of the entire log on startup** -- each of spot-perp's three Ingestors rebuilds sequence state by reading the whole file, so a 7.9 GB log costs 20+ minutes before the first socket opens. `--seconds` is the run DURATION and defaults to 1800; omitting the symbol or the duration is how the first restart attempt failed.

**The two launchd agents restart themselves at login** — `dev.gabana.genesis.disk-guard` (every
15 min) and `dev.gabana.genesis.collector-watch` (07:30, 19:30). The collector watch may fire
STALLED for q5/hl1 while they are down; that is it working, not a fault.

**crontab is wedged** (setuid root, unkillable without sudo, no sudo available) and still holds
the ECON-1 daily and LIQ-2 deep/fast jobs. Those still fire. Any *new* schedule must go through
launchd until someone runs `sudo pkill crontab`.

## 6. Standing practice adopted today

- **Salvage register** ([`SALVAGE-REGISTER.md`](SALVAGE-REGISTER.md)) — when a contract closes,
  separate the *use* that failed from the *finding* that survived. A weak instrument usually
  fails a completeness claim while remaining valid for a presence claim.
- **Baseline hierarchy** ([`next-phase-review-2026-08-19.md`](next-phase-review-2026-08-19.md) §8)
  — every future contract names a competent alternative, not only a luck null. Tier 4, Genesis
  with its epistemic machinery disabled, has never been used and is the only thing that could
  substantiate the architecture's value.
- **Check plausible premises, not just surprising results.** Today's largest error survived a full
  day because "21 days" did not sound wrong. The rule "a surprising number is a suspected bug"
  was applied to zero `withdrawable` and to a 500 KB parse failure, and never to a number nobody
  would blink at.
